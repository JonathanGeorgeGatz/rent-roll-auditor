THE RENT ROLL AUDITOR — the three prompts, exactly as used on camera (Build It With AI, EP40)

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PROMPT 1 — the naive ask (produced v1.html, preserved with its lies labeled)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Build me one self-contained HTML file for a small landlord — no internet access, no
account, no libraries fetched from anywhere, everything inline. I will paste in two CSV
exports: a rent roll with the header unit,tenant,monthly_rent,lease_start,lease_end,deposit
and a payments ledger with the header payment_id,date,unit,amount,method. The page should
read both and tell me whether the rent that should have arrived actually did — a collection
rate, a verdict, and a simple month-by-month chart.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PROMPT 2 — the grid (produced v2.html, preserved with its lies labeled)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Rebuild it month by month instead of as one total. Every lease owes every month from its
lease start date. Payments land against those months. Show me every month that stands
open: unit, tenant, month, what was paid against it and what the rent is. No more
portfolio-level averages — I want to see exactly who is behind and when.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
PROMPT 3 — the law (produced rent-roll-auditor.html, the shipped tool)
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Rewrite it under these rules, exactly:

1. Money lives as an integer count of cents, rounded half-up once at parse. Dates are ISO
   (YYYY-MM-DD) only, and impossible calendar dates are refused.
2. The audit runs through the last full calendar month ending on or before the latest valid
   payment date. Each lease owes every calendar month from lease_start through lease_end
   (or through the audit end if lease_end is blank); a mid-month start or end is prorated
   by days, rounded half-up.
3. A payment settles its unit's OLDEST unpaid month first and the overflow rolls forward —
   so rent paid early still counts, and a shortfall rides forward instead of hiding. Money
   left after every owed month is full is "credit beyond the lease", flagged, and a payment
   dated after lease_end is flagged as paid-after-lease-end.
4. A payment that exactly matches an earlier one — same unit, same date, same amount — is
   HELD as a suspected duplicate, listed by payment id, and never applied to balances.
5. A payment whose unit is not on the rent roll is an ORPHAN: listed with its dollars,
   never allocated, never guessed onto a "similar" unit.
6. Refuse what you cannot read, out loud, line by line, with the reason: bad dates,
   non-numeric or negative or zero amounts (a negative is a refund, not rent income), blank
   units or tenants, duplicate payment ids, duplicate units on the roll, rent that is not a
   positive number. Refused rows take no further part.
7. Findings at the end of the window: MISSED months (nothing paid), SHORT months (partial,
   with a forgiveness dial), LATE months (rent not fully in by the grace day — the
   completion date is the payment that brought the month to paid-in-full net of the
   forgiveness dial), and chronically late tenants. Three dials belong to me: grace day
   (late after the 4th / 6th / 11th), forgiven shortfall ($0 / $5 / $20), and how many late
   months make a tenant chronic (2 / 3 / 5).
8. Headline: scheduled rent, money applied to rent, uncollected dollars and percent. When
   uncollected passes 5% of scheduled, show a banner reading DO NOT CALL THIS COLLECTED.
9. The page audits itself on every run: rows re-tallied two ways, a conservation check
   (allocated + credit + held + orphans must equal the valid payment total), the suspect
   list recounted independently, and a refusal-rate check. If any self-check fails, or a
   file is too mangled to vouch for (more than a quarter of its rows refused, or a wrong
   header), withhold all findings and show DO NOT TRUST THIS AUDIT with the reason.
10. Everything stays on my computer: no network requests of any kind, ever.

────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
The sample portfolio ("Quillbrook Properties") is fictional. Every flaw in it was planted
on purpose so the audits could be checked against a known answer.
